Coffee brewing methods

The method matters less than getting three things right: the grind, the water and the ratio. Start with fresh beans and grind them just before brewing.

French press: use a coarse grind and a ratio of about one part coffee to fifteen parts water. Pour water that has just stopped boiling, stir once, and let it steep for four minutes before pressing slowly. Pour it all out right away, or it keeps extracting and turns bitter.

Pour-over: use a medium grind. Wet the filter first, then add the water in slow circles, in a few pours, over about three minutes. If it tastes sour the grind is too coarse; if it tastes harsh and bitter, make it a little coarser or stop sooner.

Espresso needs a very fine grind and a machine that pushes hot water through at high pressure, so most people make it at a cafe rather than at home.
