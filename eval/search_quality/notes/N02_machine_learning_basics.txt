Machine learning basics

In supervised learning, a model is shown examples with the right answers attached, such as emails marked spam or not spam, and learns a rule that predicts the answer for new examples. In unsupervised learning there are no answers, and the model looks for structure on its own, such as groups of similar customers.

Always split your data before you start. The training set is what the model learns from; the test set is held back and used once at the end, to see how the model does on examples it has never seen. Scoring a model on the data it trained on tells you very little.

The most common failure is overfitting: the model memorises the quirks and noise of the training data instead of the general pattern, so it scores very well in training and badly on new data. More data, a simpler model, and stopping training early all help. Underfitting is the opposite: the model is too simple to capture the pattern at all.
